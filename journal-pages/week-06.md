## Data Exploration

For my project, the data source will be self-collected behavioural data focused on moments when I instinctively check or unlock my phone without a strong purpose. Rather than using an existing public dataset, I will generate the data myself over several days through direct observation and recording. The data comes from my own daily habits and interactions with my phone, particularly moments linked to distraction, boredom, habit, or avoidance. This approach allows the project to focus on personal and lived experience rather than large-scale or anonymous datasets.

The data will contain a series of repeated observations collected throughout the day. Each entry will include simple information such as the approximate time, location, emotional state, and possible reason for checking the phone. Some entries may also include duration or surrounding context if it feels relevant in the moment. The structure of the data will remain intentionally lightweight and flexible rather than highly quantitative. Instead of aiming for exact accuracy, the data will function more like a behavioural trace or diary of attention. The information will likely be recorded in quick notes and later translated into a physical visual system using materials such as thread, beads, knots, or paper fragments.

One limitation of this dataset is that it relies entirely on self-observation, meaning the data will be subjective and inconsistent. There will likely be moments that I forget to record, misinterpret, or simplify. Emotional states such as boredom or distraction are also difficult to define clearly, meaning the categories may overlap or change over time. However, these gaps and ambiguities are important to the direction of the project rather than problems to eliminate. They reflect the imperfect and unstable nature of human attention, which contrasts with how digital systems often try to make behaviour appear objective and measurable.

Another limitation is that the dataset only reflects one individual experience, meaning it cannot represent wider social behaviour. However, the project is less concerned with producing universal conclusions and more focused on revealing patterns through accumulation and repetition. This limitation supports the conceptual direction of the work by keeping the data personal, embodied, and reflective.

These limitations influence the project direction by encouraging a more interpretive and material approach to data visualisation. Rather than presenting the data as scientific evidence, the project treats the collection process itself as part of the artwork. The gaps, inconsistencies, and subjective decisions become visible within the final physical structure, reinforcing ideas from data humanism and questioning how behavioural data is usually collected and interpreted in contemporary digital systems.

## Visual Design & Precedent Study

1. Giorgia Lupi & Stefanie Posavec — Dear Data

What draws me to this project is how it transforms small personal observations into hand-drawn visual systems that feel emotional and human rather than scientific. The work makes ordinary behaviours feel meaningful through careful use of symbols, colour, and repetition.

I want to carry forward the idea of subjective and imperfect data collection, where gaps and inconsistencies are treated as part of the project rather than mistakes. The project reinforces my interest in translating everyday behaviours into physical and reflective forms.

*https://www.dear-data.com/theproject*

*https://giorgialupi.com/* 

2. Nathalie Miebach — Weather Data Sculptures

I am drawn to how Nathalie Miebach transforms environmental data into woven sculptural forms that can be physically experienced. Her work turns invisible systems like weather into objects that feel tactile and spatial.

This inspires me to think about data not only as something visual but also as something material and accumulative. It reinforces my direction of creating a hanging or growing physical structure rather than a flat chart or screen-based visualisation.

*https://www.nathaliemiebach.com/*

3. Jenny Odell — How to Do Nothing

Although this is not a traditional data visualisation project, I am interested in Odell’s ideas about attention, distraction, and resisting systems of constant productivity. The project connects strongly to my focus on unconscious phone-checking and fragmented attention.

I want to carry forward the idea of paying attention to small everyday behaviours and questioning how digital systems shape human focus. This reference deepens the conceptual direction of my project and reinforces its critical perspective.

*https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/600671/how-to-do-nothing-by-jenny-odell/*

4. Lauren McCarthy — Follower

What draws me to this project is how it explores surveillance and behavioural tracking in an intimate and uncomfortable way. The work makes invisible systems of observation visible through personal experience.

This makes me think more critically about how behavioural data is collected and interpreted. It reinforces my interest in making the audience feel slightly aware or uncomfortable about how much of everyday life can become measurable data.

5. Data Strings / Knot Recording Systems / Quipu

I am interested in historical systems like quipu, where knots and strings were used to store and communicate information physically. These systems feel simple but carry layers of meaning through repetition, material, and arrangement.

I want to carry forward the use of accumulation and encoded material systems in my own project. This reference changes my direction slightly by making me think more about the physical language of the work itself, rather than only the recorded data.

## Project Planning and Skills Roadmap

![alt text](../assets/week-06/未命名作品.jpg)

What do I need to learn?

1. Developing a material encoding system

I need to experiment with how colours, spacing, knots, and different materials can represent emotional states, time gaps, or reasons for phone checking in a way that is visually readable but still open to interpretation.

2. Constructing a stable physical structure

I need to test how to physically build and present the piece using thread, pins, beads, or hanging systems. This includes understanding tension, spacing, and how the structure changes as more data is added.

3. Creating a sustainable data collection method

I need to develop a simple recording process that allows me to quickly capture moments of unconscious phone checking throughout the day without interrupting the behaviour itself.

4. Exploring installation and presentation methods

I want to test whether the work functions better as a hanging vertical string or as a stretched string-board installation. I also need to consider how viewers will move around and read the work in space.

5. Refining conceptual framing

I want to further research ideas around surveillance, behavioural tracking, data humanism, and accumulation to strengthen the conceptual meaning of the project.

What are my next steps?

My next step is to begin actively collecting behavioural data over several days, focusing specifically on unconscious moments of checking or unlocking my phone. I will test a lightweight recording method, such as quick handwritten notes or tally marks, to keep the process immediate and reflective. At the same time, I will experiment with physical materials including thread, wool, pins, beads, and small found objects to explore how different visual systems communicate repetition, emotion, and time gaps.

I also plan to prototype both directions shown in my sketch. The hanging “data string” explores accumulation vertically over time, while the string-board version focuses more on emotional fluctuation and movement through space. By testing both forms physically, I can better understand which structure communicates the concept more effectively.

Alongside material experimentation, I will continue documenting the process through sketches, photos, and written reflections in my Making Journal. Through iterative making and testing, I hope to refine both the visual language and conceptual direction of the project while allowing the final form to develop naturally from the collected data.
